Part 1

The short version

The For You feed is rebuilt per request. Your post is scored per viewer as a weighted sum of that viewer's predicted probability of taking each action on it. Then a handful of multipliers and filters decide whether it lands on screen. Nothing in the code counts followers as a score input, and nothing counts likes as such; what wins is being the post a particular person is predicted to reply to, quote, share, or follow because of.

Part 2 · Scoring

How a post is scored

Phoenix (a transformer) reads the viewer's recent action history and outputs, for each candidate post, a probability for each action. RankingScorer turns those into one number:

score = Σ weighti × Pviewer(actioni | post)   // home-mixer/scorers/ranking_scorer.rs:451

The weights below are the production defaults mirrored into home-mixer/params/param.rs (last sync 2026-08-12). Read them as multipliers on a probability, not as exchange rates between counts: a report is weighted 468× a like because reports are more than 1000× rarer than likes, so a tiny predicted probability still needs a big weight to matter at all. X added that comment to the code this week because people kept reading it the other way.

What happens when the sum goes negative. A net-negative post is squashed to a hair above zero: (score + 367.22) / 410.54 × 0.001, while any net-positive post gets score + 0.001. So a post that a viewer is predicted to mute or report is not "ranked lower"; it is placed beneath every positive post in the pool. ranking_scorer.rs:554-562, constants in params/config.rs.

Part 4 · After scoring

After the score: multipliers, boosts, and the cut

Once every candidate has a weighted score, four things happen in RankingScorer::score and the selectors, in this order: a one-post cold-start lift, an author-diversity decay, an out-of-network multiplier, and a top-K cut. Then a separate service reorders the top of the list for variety.

1. Cold-start lift: one small-account post per request gets a free ride to slot ~15

Every For You request picks exactly one eligible post and raises its score to whatever the 15th/16th-ranked post scored (random slot in [15, 16), so slot 15). Eligibility, all required (scorers/author_cold_start.rs:138-143, 273-284, params in params/param.rs:646-682):

  • author has ≤ 1,000 followers (ColdStartFollowerCap)
  • the post is an original: not a reply, not a repost
  • the post has < 1,000 impressions so far (ColdStartImpressionThreshold, read from the live view count)
  • the post is already ranked inside the top 85% of the non-zero pool (LowImpressionsMaxPositionRatio 0.85), i.e. it can't be junk
  • in the treatment arm, the post is under 24 hours old (ColdStartMaxPostAgeSecs 86400)

Among the eligible, the highest-scoring one wins (Thompson sampling is coded but off). Practical reading: while you're under 1k followers, each fresh original post competes for one guaranteed mid-feed slot per viewer request, but only against other small-account posts in that viewer's pool, and only if the model already likes it for that viewer. That's your best structural break; it disappears at 1,001 followers.

2. Author diversity: your second post in the same pool is worth 62.5%

Within one ranked pool, the k-th post from the same author is multiplied by (1 − 0.25) × 0.5k + 0.25 (ranking_scorer.rs:643-645; AuthorDiversityDecay 0.5, AuthorDiversityFloor 0.25):

Post from you in this poolMultiplier
1st1.000
2nd0.625
3rd0.438
4th0.344
5th and beyond→ 0.25 floor

Posting a lot doesn't get you punished, but it doesn't stack either: per viewer session, roughly one of your posts gets full weight and the rest fight at a discount. Spread posts out so different sessions see different "first" posts, and make each one able to stand alone.

3. Out-of-network discount: 0.75×, and it also hits your replies and reposts

Posts from authors the viewer doesn't follow are multiplied by OonWeightFactor = 0.75. The same 0.75 is applied to replies and reposts from accounts the viewer does follow (EnableOonRescoreForInNetworkRepliesRetweets = true, ranking_scorer.rs:773-783). Topic-filtered feeds use 0.5. So an original post is the only format that ever gets the full 1.0 to your own followers; a repost or reply is discounted before it even competes.

4. The cut: 50 survive, 35 are shown

TopKScoreSelector keeps the top 50 by final score (TOP_K_CANDIDATES_TO_SELECT = 50), the response holds 35 posts plus 4 module slots (RESULT_SIZE = 35, params/config.rs). Candidates older than 48 hours never enter the pool (MAX_POST_AGE = 48 * 60 * 60, AgeFilter). Who-to-Follow sits at position 6, prompts at 0, the feed survey at 12 when enabled.

5. The DPP reranker: near-duplicate takes get dropped

VMRanker sends the top 150 to a separate service that runs a determinantal point process over each post's content embedding (the same multimodal embedding behind semantic IDs), with theta = 0.65 (vm-ranker/scoring/dpp_model.rs, vm-ranker/dpp.rs:87-122). It greedily picks up to 50 posts, preferring high score and low similarity to what it already picked; survivors keep their score, everyone else is zeroed. Rough numbers from the formula: a top-scored post that is 0.9-cosine-similar to an already-picked post is beaten by any distinct post scoring ≥ 11% of the top score. If several posts in a viewer's pool say the same thing (same news, same meme, same clip), about one survives.

6. Threads, reposts, and "one slot per conversation"

  • Only post 1 of a thread can reach non-followers. Out-of-network replies and reposts are removed before scoring (OONRetweetReplyFilter, filters/oon_retweet_reply_filter.rs:13-18). Quote posts are treated as originals and survive.
  • For your followers, parts 2..n are replies: they get the 0.75× factor, no mutual-follow reply boost, no cold-start lift, and DedupConversationFilter keeps one item per conversation per page (the highest scored), rendered as root + at most one intermediate + the focal post (filters/dedup_conversation_filter.rs:42-49, util/urt/convo_marshaller.rs). A thread never spreads across a page.
  • Reposts by others are how you cross into new follower graphs. A repost is in-network for the reposter's followers, so it enters their pool as a followed-account item (at 0.75×). Multiple reposts of the same post collapse to one per viewer (RetweetDeduplicationFilter), and age is judged on the repost's own ID, so a fresh repost re-enters the 48h window.
  • A post gets one shot per viewer: seen and served IDs (post, its original, and its parent) are excluded; "served" persists 10 min / 100 IDs (filters/previously_seen_posts_filter.rs, filters/previously_served_posts_filter.rs). Posts ranked 36-100 are cached for 3 minutes and typically show on the viewer's next page.

7. Ads and brand safety decide placement, not survival

The default ads blender (partition_organic_low_risk) never places a "MediumRisk" post next to an ad and never lets one take position 0. MediumRisk means: any NSFW / gore / do-not-amplify / malicious-URL label, or simply not yet scored for brand safety by Grok (models/brand_safety.rs:40-63). Clean, already-scored posts are the ones eligible for the top slot; each ad also pushes one organic post off the 35-item page.

Following tab is not ranked. "Following" is reverse-chronological with only dedup and safety filters (candidate_pipeline/reverse_chron_posts_pipeline.rs); "Ranked Following" runs the same scorer over followed accounts only. Everything in this manual is about For You.

Part 5 · Retrieval

Reaching people who don't follow you

Out-of-network posts come from two sources queried in parallel: Phoenix retrieval (a two-tower model over an index of recent posts, up to 1,000 candidates per request) and SimClusters (engagement-cluster similarity, up to 800). Both are then scored by the same model as your followers' posts, then multiplied by 0.75. Getting into those two pools is the whole game for reach.

The Phoenix index has a door, and it closes at 24 hours

The production For You retrieval index is called 1fav_1day ("HOME"). A post is admitted only if all of the following hold (phoenix-rankall-strato/…/phoenixRankAllCandidateProcessor.strato:381-487, retention in phoenix-rankall/src/config/mod.rs:139-191):

  • it is an original post: replies, reposts, and community posts are skipped outright
  • it passes visibility filtering evaluated with no viewer at the "home recommendations" safety level, and is not NSFW-annotated (so any spam / NSFW / malicious-URL / do-not-amplify label on you or the post keeps it out entirely)
  • it has received at least one like (the like event is processed ~2 minutes after it happens; further re-indexing fires at power-of-two like counts: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32…)
  • it is less than 24 hours old, measured from creation, and it is evicted at 24h no matter how well it is doing

Ranking's own hard cap is 48 hours (AgeFilter). So the effective life of a post for discovery is one day, with the first hours mattering most because the model also sees post age in 60-minute buckets as an input.

What the model actually knows about your post

The shipped production ranking config (home_direct_packed) builds each candidate token from: hashed post ID, hashed author ID, product surface, post-age bucket, the viewer's timezone and local hour, and semantic IDs (phoenix/xrex/models/recsys_feature_prep.py:516-724). Semantic IDs are quantized codes of a multimodal embedding of the post produced by a Qwen3-VL embedding model that is fed: author display name and handle, the post text with t.co links stripped, images, video frames, link-card title and description, poll choices, and quoted-post text/images (phoenix/reference/mm_encoder.py).

The model sees
Content meaning (via semantic IDs) · who you are (author hash) · how old the post is · the viewer's last ~1,022 actions with dwell times · viewer country, language, geo, apps, age bracket
The model does not see
Raw text · like/repost/reply counts (enable_engagement_counts = False) · your follower count · verified / Premium status · post language · a "has link" or "has media" flag · bookmarks (no ranking weight)

Two consequences. First, engagement counts are not a ranking input; they gate index admission (≥1 like) and the cold-start lift (<1,000 views), that's all. "Social proof" works on humans, not on Phoenix. Second, since content enters as meaning, the post has to be legibly about something: clear topic, clear stance, and if you attach a link, the card's title/description is what gets embedded, not the URL.

Retrieval matches your identity + content against the viewer's history

The retrieval two-tower represents each indexed post as hashed author ID + semantic IDs only (phoenix/xrex/models/recsys_two_tower_model.py:1141-1172), trained with likes as positives and reports / not-interested / block / mute / unfollow as hard negatives. A brand-new author's hash row is untrained, so retrieval leans entirely on content; an established author's posts inherit "who likes this author". Being consistently about one thing is how the author hash becomes a useful signal.

SimClusters: the second door, and it only counts likes

SimClusters seeds from the viewer's own recent engagements (up to 15 each of likes, reposts, replies, bookmarks, shares, own posts, plus photo expands and quality video views) and finds posts whose engagement-cluster vector is similar (cosine > 0.5, posts up to 48h old, home-mixer/sources/simclusters_source.rs:24-35). How your post gets a cluster vector at all (simclusters/…/summingbird/storm/TweetJob.scala, Configs.scala):

  • Only likes build it (ServerTweetFav); replies, quotes, reposts, bookmarks and views do nothing here. Self-likes are excluded.
  • Each like adds the liker's own cluster interests, but only clusters where that liker's interest score is ≥ 0.3. So a like from someone deeply inside "AI builders" moves you into that cluster; a like from a random account with no clear interests adds nothing.
  • The cluster score halves every 8 hours without new likes; likes on posts older than 3 days are ignored; you must sit in the cluster's served top 200 to be retrievable.
  • A post needs ≥ 8 likes before it gets a persistent embedding and can act as a seed for other viewers.
  • Authors flagged for adult content are dropped from this source for non-followers.

Practical reading: who likes your post in the first hours matters more than how many. Ten likes from people who are clearly inside your lane put you in front of the rest of that lane; a hundred likes from a giveaway crowd put you nowhere in particular.

Reputation (user-cred-v2) is a shield, not a booster

The PageRank score in user-cred-v2/ is not read by home-mixer, Phoenix, SimClusters, or Thunder at all. It's consumed by the spam and enforcement systems: a score ≥ 54 (botmaker-rules/scarecrow/derived-feature/IsHighPageRankUser.df) or ≥ 50 (abuse-enforcement-service/…/enforcement_post.yaml) exempts you from most automatic spam / NSFW / low-quality-URL labels and enforcement. Mass flows to you from being followed by high-mass accounts that follow few others, and from likes and reposts by high-mass accounts within rolling 7-day windows; Premium/verified accounts get a uniform share of the teleport probability; linked alt accounts are edge-stripped and do nothing. Follower count is not an input (only a >25,000-follower fallback when unscored). Build it slowly, by being liked by people who matter in your lane.

Part 6 · Visibility

What suppresses you

Ranking decides order. Visibility filtering decides whether a post can be shown at all, and it is a readable list of rules over labels (visibility-filtering/rules/registry.rs). The rules run in order; the first Drop ends evaluation. One set applies to everyone; a second, stricter set applies only when the post is a recommendation from an account the viewer doesn't follow. That second set is where "my followers see it but nobody else does" comes from. And because you are exempt from your own labels when viewing your own posts, you can't see it happening from your account.

Rules that apply to everyone (selected)

  • Protected account → zero reach beyond followers (ProtectedAuthorDropRule).
  • Tweet label SPAM (a link to a "BAD"-verdict URL) → dropped for everyone including followers. Policy labels (hateful conduct, violent speech, abuse, civic integrity, PDNA, bounce) likewise.
  • Edited posts: only the latest version is eligible (DropStaleTweetsRule). Withheld-by-country and DMCA'd media drop for affected viewers.
  • Sensitive-media posts (NSFW / gore labels, or your own "sensitive" flag) are dropped for logged-out viewers, under-18 viewers, and viewers with no stated age in 16 countries; shown behind an interstitial to followers who haven't opted in.

Rules that apply only to non-follower recommendations

These are the ones that quietly cap reach. Post labels: SPAM_HIGH_RECALL, MALICIOUS_URL, DO_NOT_AMPLIFY, NSFW_HIGH_RECALL, NSFW_HIGH_PRECISION (even for opted-in viewers), NSFW_TEXT, NSFW_CARD_IMAGE, GORE_AND_VIOLENCE_HIGH_PRECISION, FOSNR_ABUSE_INSULTS. Account labels that hide all your posts from non-followers: SpamHighRecall, any Nsfw* account label, NsfwAvatarImage, NsfwBannerImage, Compromised, ReadOnly, ImpersonationHighPrecision, AbusiveHighRecall, DoNotAmplify; plus the account-level "mark media as sensitive" setting.

Two mechanics people don't expect. (1) The out-of-network retrieval index applies these rules with no viewer (i.e. as logged-out), so any sensitive-media or spam-labeled post never enters the index in the first place. (2) When you reply under or quote a post, its ancestors and the quoted post are evaluated with the stricter policy, and if any of them is dropped, your post is dropped too, even for your own followers (AncillaryVFFilter, home-mixer/candidate_hydrators/vf_candidate_hydrator.rs:139-178). Don't build on labeled accounts.

Under the Hood: check, don't guess

x.com/i/under_the_hood shows the labels applied to your account and posts for the prior calendar month, per label, if your account is ≥ 365 days old and had ≥ 10 original posts that month (report available ~10 days after month end; under-the-hood/strato/columns/underTheHoodReport.User.strato:15-19). It surfaces exactly the FY-relevant labels above (post: SPAM_HIGH_RECALL, SPAM, MALICIOUS_URL, DO_NOT_AMPLIFY, the NSFW/gore set, FOSNR; account: SpamHighRecall, the Nsfw* set, Compromised, ReadOnly, ImpersonationHighPrecision, AbusiveHighRecall, DoNotAmplify). Non-zero rows point at specific posts and links; a clean report plus low reach means the cause is ranking, not filtering.

Part 7

Myths vs. the code

The claims going around this week, checked against the repo. "Not in the repo" means a full-tree search found nothing; the withheld pieces (Grox prompts, some botmaker rules) are the only place such a thing could hide.

"One report cancels 468 likes"WrongWeights multiply each viewer's predicted probability, not counts. X added the comment at param.rs:279-307 to say exactly this. Reports are >1000× rarer than likes; the −234 exists so the prediction can matter at all, and it's personalized.

Part 8 · Operating plan

Your operating plan

Everything above reduces to one sentence: publish original posts that a specific kind of person is predicted to reply to, quote, forward, or follow because of, keep every one of them clean and legibly on one topic, and get the first like and the first real replies from people inside your lane within the first hours. Below is how to run that, in order of leverage. The examples assume a builder-in-public / AI-tools lane; the mechanics don't change with the lane, only the examples. To get this plan tailored to your own account, use the xboss skill: it reads your posts and writes the diagnosis and the drafts for you.

1. Pick one lane and stay in it for 90 days

Retrieval represents your post as author hash + content codes. A scattered account has an author hash that predicts nothing, so it lives on content alone; a consistent account accumulates "the people who like this author like this kind of thing" and gets pulled into the right histories. Concretely: 80% of originals inside the lane (building AI tools, what the code of X/Claude/etc. actually does, shipping in public), 20% adjacent. Write the bio to match, pin the best lane post.

2. Design each original for one high-weight action

  • Forward-worthy (copy link 20, DM 5, share 2): reference posts. "The 9 constants in X's ranking file that matter." "Every filter that removes a post from For You, in order." Checklists, decoded configs, before/after numbers, a tool people can open. These are what people send to a group chat.
  • Reply-worthy (5, or 20 from mutuals): a specific claim plus a specific question. "The 0.75 out-of-network factor also hits your reposts to your own followers. Did anyone expect that?" Ask something a reader can answer in one line.
  • Quote-worthy (5): a template or a strong take people want to attach their own version to. "Reply-to-like ratio on your last 10 posts, go." Quotes are originals, so they travel and they count for the quoter's audience.
  • Follow-worthy (4): visible series and shipped things. "Day 3 of reading X's ranking code." "Shipped: the simulator." The follow head is P(viewer follows you because of this post); a post that promises a next one lifts it.
  • Dwell (0.004/s): small but real: 30 s of predicted dwell ≈ a quarter of a like. Long, dense posts that reward reading also lower not-dwelled. Never pad; the model sees meaning, not length.

3. Build the mutual-follow reply graph, deliberately

The +15 reply boost applies only when the viewer follows you and you follow them back, on your originals. Rules: follow back every account that replies substantively; each day, leave 5-10 real replies on originals from people in your lane (their followers see the conversation module: root + your reply, at 0.75×; and it makes you a mutual over time); keep a private list of 20-30 mutuals who actually reply and reciprocate on their posts. This is the legitimate version of an "engagement pod": mutuals with genuinely high P(reply).

4. The first three hours, every original

  1. Post at a time when your lane's audience is active for you: for an India + Europe + US-tech mix, roughly 6-8pm IST or 8-10am ET; the model sees the viewer's local hour as context, so it's about who is awake, not a "best time" penalty.
  2. Make sure the first like lands within minutes; that opens the retrieval door for the next ~24h. This is the one place a friend's like actually matters structurally.
  3. DM the post to 3-5 mutuals for whom it is genuinely relevant. A reply from an account with a real following carries your post as a conversation module into their followers' feeds (in-network for them). A quote is better still: it's an original for their audience.
  4. Reply to every substantive reply, then stop. Your own replies under your post are worth nothing beyond your followers.
  5. Do not repost or reply-bump the post yourself; a self-repost is deduped against the original and gets 0.75×; your replies collapse into one conversation slot anyway.

5. Cadence and spacing

  • Under 1,000 followers: 2 originals per day, spaced ≥ 4 hours apart at times your lane is awake, so there is always a fresh <24h original with <1,000 views competing for the cold-start slot, without your posts competing against each other in the same session (author diversity: 2nd post ×0.625). Every original gets the first-like protocol; skip a slot rather than post something without a payload.
  • Over 1,000: 1-2 originals per day is enough; quality of the predicted actions dominates. Reposts and replies are for community, not reach.
  • Threads only when the depth is genuinely for followers; put the payload in post 1. Prefer a standalone follow-up original the next day (it re-enters the 24h window; a quote of your own earlier post keeps the context and is still an original).
  • Same news as everyone else: only with a distinct angle (the DPP will pick one version per pool).

6. Keep the account clean (this is most of "shadowban")

Ranking decides order; visibility filtering decides existence, and it runs on labels produced by rule engines and models that mostly watch links and behaviour, not opinions (Part 6). The checklist, in order of how likely each is to bite you:

  • Only link to reputable domains you'd put your name on; never shorteners or affiliate redirect chains (a LOW_QUALITY hop hides the post from non-followers; a BAD verdict drops it for everyone, retroactively).
  • Never pin a post with a link unless the domain is unimpeachable (a pinned bad link hides your whole account from non-followers for a week).
  • Don't @mention people who don't follow you in a post that has a link; keep mentions under two per post.
  • No engagement bait ("like if…", "RT to…", "comment YES"): the LLM classifier labels it SpamHighRecall regardless of reputation.
  • Don't reply under or quote accounts that look labeled (spammy, adult, mass-reported): their drop becomes your drop, even for your followers.
  • No copy-paste replies, no reposting the same text, no LLM slop at volume.
  • No rage bait or targeted insults: FOSNR_ABUSE_INSULTS hides the post from non-followers, and predicted mute (−58.8) / not-interested (−43.2) quietly sink the score with everyone else.
  • Nothing borderline-adult in media, avatar, banner, or link-card image; don't turn on "mark media as sensitive".
  • Don't automate: no bursts of actions under a second apart, no follow/unfollow or like/unlike cycles, no engaging with posts you never opened.
  • Don't go protected; don't rely on editing (only the latest version is eligible).

Check x.com/i/under_the_hood monthly for labels on your account and posts.

8. Thirty days

Days 1-7 · the algorithm series

One original per day from the templates above, each ending in a specific question, plus 5-10 real replies daily on lane originals. Follow back every substantive replier. Publish the simulator link once, on Day 4, after making the artifact public from its share menu.

Days 8-14 · turn the audience into a lane

Shift to your own building: what you shipped, what a config or codebase actually does, decoded. Keep the "I read the source" format; it is your differentiator against the take-merchants and it embeds distinctly. Two originals a day if under 1k followers.

Days 15-30 · compound

Sequel every post that beat your median on shares or replies. Quote your own best originals with new data instead of threading. Keep the mutual list to 30 people who reply. Review Under the Hood at day 30.
